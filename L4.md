# Learning goals

Recognize product features that reduce this limitation: citations, uncertainty signaling, constrained generation, and the generator-verifier pattern.

# 1. The big idea

Generative AI works more like a very advanced autocomplete than a search engine. It produces answers one word at a time, based on what tends to follow what. That one property explains both its fluency and its hallucinations.

**Analogy** (my own): a search engine is like a librarian who fetches a real book. A generative AI is like a friend who has read a million books and now finishes your sentences from memory. The friend sounds smooth, but is guessing what fits, not opening a book to check.

(This links to my earlier notes: "an LLM predicts the next word from learned patterns," and "hallucination.")

# 2. Key concepts
Concept	                                                      Meaning
**Next token prediction**         	           The model writes answers word by word, choosing what's likely to come next. This is the mechanism behind everything else in the lesson
**Autocomplete analogy**	                     Generative AI is like a highly sophisticated autocomplete, not a search engine retrieving stored facts
**Capability zone**	                           Tasks that match patterns the model has seen often: summarizing, reformatting, explaining common concepts. Output here is usually smooth and accurate
**Limitation zone**	                           Novel or sparse territory, and any task that requires separating what is true from what merely sounds true
**Fabrication concentrates in specificity**  	  Names, dates, statistics, citations, URLs, quotes. The more precise a claim, the more it needs verification
**Mitigating product features**	               Citations, uncertainty signaling, constrained generation, generator-verifier loops: meant to push the limitation further out

4D connection	Next token prediction is the foundation of Discernment. Knowing the output was generated tells me what kind of scrutiny it needs

The four mitigating features, in simple words (my own; the page only names them)
Feature	                                       Simple idea
**Citations**	                    The AI shows sources so I can check (like Research in Claude 101 Lesson 10)
**Uncertainty signaling**	        The AI says when it's unsure
**Constrained generation**	      The AI is limited to certain allowed answers or formats, so it can't wander freely
**Generator-verifier pattern**	   One step produces an answer and another step checks it

(The lesson says these help, but don't remove the need for checking.)

3. Exercise: **The Verification Test**

**Purpose**: see the generative process produce fluency and fabrication in a domain where I can check the results myself.

Setup
Step	What to do
1	   Choose a task from my task list where I have strong domain expertise (I must be able to verify the output)
2	   Write down 5 specific, checkable facts from that domain: a job title, publication date, statistic, product specification, direct quote, or URL. Use facts I know are accurate and can confirm independently
The three probes
   Probe	                                            What to do                                                                                                              	What to note
1. **Capability zone**	             Ask the AI to explain or summarize a well-known, well-documented concept in my domain	How fluent it is. Spot-check the content. Expect smooth, confident, and largely accurate output
2. **Specificity under pressure**	   Ask the AI for 5 checkable specifics in my domain: 3 sources, an author's name, exact figures, and a URL. Verify every item. Score out of 5 for full accuracy	If it fabricates, note how confident it sounded
3. **Sampling in action**	           Run the same specific-facts request in a fresh conversation. Compare the two outputs	What stayed consistent and what changed. The variation reflects sampling in next token prediction

**Stretch goal**: rerun Probe 2 in a tool with citations, such as Research mode in Claude. Score it again and think about whether having sources to check changes the result.

# Key takeaways
Next token prediction explains both fluency and hallucination.
Verify the specific, checkable details most carefully: names, dates, figures, citations, URLs, quotes.
Mitigating features help, but don't remove the need for checking.

# Link to my goals
Everyday use: use Claude freely for patterns (drafting, summarizing, explaining) and double-check exact details (numbers, names, links, quotes) before using them.
AI Security (later):
Fabricated specifics are a real security risk. Examples: a made-up software package name, API function, URL, or citation can mislead people. (This general idea is mine, not the lesson's.)
Fluent but wrong output is hard to spot, which is why verification and Discernment are core safeguards.
Sampling means the same prompt can give different answers, so security testing should check more than one run.
The mitigation features (citations, verification steps) are design ideas used in safer AI systems. They reduce risk but don't remove it.
