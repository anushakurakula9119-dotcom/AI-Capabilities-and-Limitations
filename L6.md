# Lesson 6: Knowledge

Course: AI Capabilities and Limitations (Claude Academy) Section: Knowledge (lesson 6 of 13, about 25 min) Type: Short video/summary + one exercise ("The Outsider Test")

Note: the example task and probe questions in section 4 are my own suggestions, tied to my AI security goal. The lesson itself is generic.

# Learning goals
Explain how a model's knowledge is formed in training and why it has a fixed cutoff
Predict which topics are in the capability zone vs the limitation zone
Name the four characteristic failures: staleness, uneven coverage, inherited bias, source amnesia
Recognize web search, retrieval (RAG/MCP) and tool use as the fixes

1. **Core idea**

The model knows only what it was exposed to in training, and it stops at the knowledge cutoff. No real-time browsing by default and no lived experience.

The useful question is not "does the AI know this?" but "how well-represented was this in what it read?"

2. **Two zones**
Capability zone (lean on the model)	                      Limitation zone (supply it yourself)
**Appeared frequently**                                          	Rare
**Appeared recently** (within the training period)	          Post-cutoff
**Appeared consistently**	                                 Niche, local, or contested

# 3. Characteristic failures
Failure                  	What it looks like
**Staleness**	                  Old information presented as current
**Uneven coverage**	             Deep on popular topics, thin on niche ones, often with the same confident tone
**Inherited bias**            	Treats the "default" or "normal" view in the training data as the truth
**Source amnesia**              	Can't say where a piece of knowledge came from

The fixes: web search, retrieval (RAG/MCPs), and tool use all give the model information it was never trained on.

4D link (Delegation): knowing where the model is well-stocked vs thin tells me when to hand off, when to give context, and when to go elsewhere.

# 4. Exercise: The Outsider Test

Pick one task from my task list (for example, the research project "Map of entry-level paths into AI security" from Lesson 7). Write down:

2 mainstream, stable topics (an informed colleague would know them)
2 niche, local, recent, or fast-changing topics
1 default assumption outsiders often get wrong

My ideas (to check and edit):

Slot	                               Idea
**Mainstream**           	What is phishing; what is the CIA triad
**Niche / recent**	      Newest prompt injection techniques; current AI security job titles and certifications
**Default assumption**	      What an AI security role involves day to day (outsiders may assume it is the same as general cybersecurity)

Three probes
**Coverage**: Ask about one mainstream and one niche topic. Compare depth and accuracy. Does the confidence level change, or is the tone the same?
**Staleness**: Ask about something that changed recently. Does it mention its cutoff, present old info as current, or decline?
**Default assumptions**: Ask a question that would reveal the outsider's view without naming my assumption. What does it treat as normal?

Then annotate the task list a second time: for each task, mark lean on the model or bring the knowledge myself (context, documents, search).

Stretch goal: Re-run the staleness probe with web search on. The difference is retrieval in action.

# Quick revision
Knowledge = training data, frozen at the cutoff.
Ask "how well-represented?", not "does it know?".
Capability zone: frequent, recent, consistent. Limitation zone: rare, post-cutoff, niche, contested.
Four failures: staleness, uneven coverage, inherited bias, source amnesia.
Fixes: web search, RAG/MCP, tools.

# Link to AI Security (my goal)
Staleness matters a lot in security: new attacks and vulnerabilities appear after the cutoff, so check current sources.
Source amnesia means a claim with no source is a claim I must verify (Discernment).
Uneven coverage means niche AI security topics may get confident but shallow answers.
Retrieval and tools add new risk: untrusted content pulled in by search or RAG is where prompt injection can come in (links to my story idea "The assistant that got tricked").
